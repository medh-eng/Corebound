Delivery note — T-001: Huxley's first message, nine memory logs and five SARATHI reactions. Draft for Director review; no runtime data changed. Logs 01–05 are pre-crash Custodian recordings; 06 and 09 are Huxley; 07–08 are corrupted SARATHI records. Where describes where a recording is found, not where it was recorded. Pre-crash readings refer to remote observations, not visits to Jivagraha. Log 07 deliberately hints at intervention without identifying an actor or cause. The few-centuries estimate in log 09 is uncertain, as requested; it does not explain the worlds' origin.

## huxley_01
Speaker: Prof. Orion Huxley
Where: escape module, main deck
Text: Custodian. My name is Orion Huxley. I am the chief scientist of Project Amarnath. If this recording has reached you, something has gone wrong, and I have failed to give you these instructions in person. I am sorry. There should have been time for a proper conversation. First, check your oxygen. Sit down if you need to. The next task can wait until you can breathe comfortably. There is a translator in the front deck. Find it, and keep it with you. We prepared for a journey through unfamiliar places; understanding what you find will matter as much as any tool. The ship's manual is in the control room. It explains the mission, your responsibilities, and the decisions recorded under my name. The control room was badly damaged. You may need another way in. Please do not mistake a missing answer for an instruction to stop asking. You deserved those answers before we left. For now, take one careful step. Then another.

## log_01
Speaker: The Custodian (voice note)
Where: near the crash site, beneath a loose escape-module panel
Title: A Window Left Open
Text: Earth is gone. I can say that to a recorder, apparently. I cannot yet say it to another person. Before departure, I kept reaching for a weather report. Rain over the coast. Wind in the trees outside the laboratory. I have saved the sound of that rain. It seems a very small thing to carry, until I try to leave it behind.

## log_02
Speaker: The Custodian (voice note)
Where: near the base, inside a displaced storage case
Title: The Smallest Cargo
Text: The manifest calls them the Seeds of Humanity. I checked the seals twice and wrote down the readings by hand. Huxley thanked me as though I had done something extraordinary. All I did was follow the checklist. Perhaps that is what care looks like when the thing you are caring for cannot tell you what it needs.

## log_03
Speaker: The Custodian (voice note)
Where: beside the shelter, in an escape-module maintenance pouch
Title: What Custodian Means
Text: My title sounds grander than the job feels. Check the stores. Keep the instruments working. Notice when a number changes. Ask why. I am a scientist entrusted with things that must arrive safely, not someone who knows every answer. I have put that in a recording because I suspect I will need reminding.

## log_04
Speaker: The Custodian (voice note)
Where: underground, in a shallow pocket beside a buried hull fragment
Title: The Readings Disagree
Text: The remote survey returned two different ages for the same patch of ground. I checked the calibration against the ship's reference sample. It was fine. I checked again because I wanted it not to be fine. Huxley asked me to keep both results. Usually he wants the clean version of a report. Today he asked for the uncertainty.

## log_05
Speaker: The Custodian (voice note)
Where: deep underground, beside a root-threaded piece of recording equipment
Title: A Familiar Rhythm
Text: There is a repeating pattern in the forest survey. I noticed it because I had just been looking at the Quantum Core monitor. They are not identical, but I keep placing the traces beside each other. Huxley says resemblance is not evidence of a connection. He is right. I wish he had looked less troubled when he said it.

## log_06
Speaker: Prof. Orion Huxley
Where: underground, inside a buried instrument case
Title: Keep Both Records
Text: The Quantum Core readings should be considered incomplete until we recover the missing reference interval. Do not smooth the irregularities out of the report. A tidy line can hide the very thing we need to understand. I have initialled the calibration sheet. I would prefer that my initials meant certainty. Tonight they mean only that I was here, and checked.

## log_07
Speaker: SARATHI (corrupted system record)
Where: underground, in an escape-module navigation fragment
Title: An Interrupted Command
Text: Navigation record recovered. Course correction requested. Authorisation field: unreadable. Correction acknowledged before the first instability warning. Sequence verified against two independent clocks. Accident classification: suspended pending review. The record establishes an intervention, not its purpose. I cannot tell you who issued the command. I cannot tell you why. The remaining silence is a missing file, not an answer.

## log_08
Speaker: SARATHI (corrupted system record)
Where: deep underground, near the Root Guardian's outer roots
Title: Roots Around The Signal
Text: Survey record recovered. A signal resembling the Core's operating rhythm appears beneath the canopy. Root response follows it in several samples. Direction of cause: undetermined. Instrument interference remains possible. The forest seems to know when the signal changes. That sentence is unsuitable for a technical report. I have retained it beside the measurements, for reasons I cannot fully recover.

## log_09
Speaker: Prof. Orion Huxley
Where: near the Root Guardian, in a recorder caught between exposed roots
Title: Younger Than The Silence
Text: The readings say this forest is only a few centuries old. I have written those words three times. Each time they look less plausible beside the scale of the canopy. Our remote dating method may be wrong; we have no local reference series. Keep that limitation attached to the estimate. And keep the estimate. Disbelief is not a correction.
