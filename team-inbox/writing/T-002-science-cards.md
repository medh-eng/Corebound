## shelter_dome
Title: A small atmosphere of your own
Card: A shelter is more than a roof. A sealed habitat must keep a suitable pressure, supply oxygen, remove carbon dioxide and control temperature and moisture. Its systems need energy and regular attention. Corebound simplifies those jobs into shelter oxygen and power so you can follow the essentials. Supplying oxygen alone would not make a real sealed room habitable indefinitely: the air people breathe out also needs processing.

Real world: Space-station life-support systems manage air, water and other conditions needed by their crews.

## solar_lotus
Title: Electricity from daylight
Card: A solar cell turns some of the energy in light directly into electrical energy. Its semiconductor material helps separate electric charges, and a connected circuit lets them flow. The lotus shape is a design choice; the useful work happens in its cells. Output falls when less light reaches them. Panels do not burn fuel while operating, but mining, manufacturing and eventual recycling still have environmental costs.
Equation: —
Real world: Silicon photovoltaic cells are used in rooftop solar panels.

## battery
Title: Keeping energy for later
Card: A rechargeable battery stores energy through chemical changes. Charging drives reactions in one direction; discharge allows reactions that supply current to a circuit. It moves energy through time rather than creating it. Some energy becomes heat during charging and use, so real storage is never perfectly efficient. Corebound uses batteries to carry daytime power into the night. Responsible material sourcing and recycling help reduce their environmental footprint.
Equation: —
Real world: Rechargeable batteries store electricity in phones, electric vehicles and grid storage systems.

## electrolyzer
Title: Splitting water with electricity
Card: An electrolyzer uses electrical energy to split water into hydrogen and oxygen. Ice must first become liquid water or steam in a suitable system; frozen crystals do not turn directly into breathable air by magic. Corebound bundles that preparation into one machine. The oxygen supports the shelter, while hydrogen is a useful product of the real reaction. How clean the process is depends strongly on how its electricity is generated.
Equation: 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂
Real world: Water electrolysis is used to produce oxygen aboard the International Space Station.

## smelter
Title: Freeing iron from its ore
Card: Iron in an ore is often chemically joined to oxygen. Heating it with a suitable reducing agent removes that oxygen, leaving metal that can be shaped. In conventional ironmaking, carbon helps generate carbon monoxide, which reduces iron oxides. This produces carbon dioxide, while handling and heating can release dust. Corebound compresses several industrial steps into one smelter. Cleaner energy, dust capture and alternative reducing agents can reduce the impacts.
Equation: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
Real world: Blast furnaces use carbon-based reduction to produce iron from iron ores.

## auto_drill

Automated operation still needs monitoring and maintenance in real mines. Dust, disturbed ground and waste rock need careful management. Corebound gives its auto-drills a continuing supply beneath them; real mineral deposits are finite and eventually become uneconomic or exhausted.

Real world: Automated drilling equipment is used in modern mines to make controlled, repeatable holes.

## refinery
Title: Turning mixed material into useful metal
Card: A refinery separates or purifies useful materials from a mixture. For metals, the route depends on the ore: heat, chemical treatment and electrical processes may all be involved. There is no universal recipe that works for every metal. Corebound groups these industrial choices into readable production steps. Wastewater, gases and solid residues need treatment; recycling existing metal can reduce demand for fresh ore, though it still needs energy.
Equation: —
Real world: Copper can be purified by electrolytic refining for uses that need high electrical conductivity.

## silicon_furnace
Title: From silica to silicon
Card: Silica contains silicon bonded to oxygen. An electric furnace can use carbon and intense heat to remove the oxygen and produce silicon. The simplified overall reaction also produces carbon monoxide, so gas handling matters. Solar cells and computer chips require further purification beyond this first furnace stage. Corebound shortens that chain for play. Cleaner electricity and controlled exhaust reduce impacts, but the furnace does not make pollution disappear.
Equation: SiO₂ + 2C → Si + 2CO
Real world: Industrial silicon production uses electric furnaces to process quartz with carbon-containing materials.

## coal_burner
Title: Power with a visible cost
Card: Burning carbon releases stored chemical energy as heat. A real power station uses that heat through equipment such as a steam turbine and generator to produce electricity. Carbon dioxide forms during complete combustion; real coal can also produce ash and other pollutants. Corebound represents this chain with one compact machine. Pollution controls can reduce some emissions, but switching to cleaner energy is different from simply hiding the smoke.
Equation: C + O₂ → CO₂
Real world: Coal-fired power stations commonly use steam turbines to drive electrical generators.

## bio_filter
Title: Cleaning with living chemistry
Card: A bio-filter uses living organisms, often microbes attached to a surface, to transform particular pollutants. The organisms need suitable conditions, and the filter must be designed for the material being treated. It is not a universal sponge for every harmful substance. Corebound simplifies this into a machine that improves air conditions. In reality, remaining gases, used filter material and maintenance needs must still be considered when assessing the system.

Real world: Biological filters are used to treat certain odorous gases from industrial and waste-handling facilities.

## settling_pond
Title: Giving particles time to settle
Card: Cloudy water often contains tiny solid particles carried along with the flow. Slowing the water in a pond gives some of those particles time to sink under gravity. Clearer water can then be separated from the accumulated sediment. This does not remove every dissolved chemical or make water automatically safe to drink. The collected material needs responsible handling, and the pond must be maintained so storms do not carry it away.
Equation: —
Real world: Sedimentation tanks are used in water and wastewater treatment.

## sabatier_reactor
Title: Recovering water and making methane
Card: The Sabatier reaction combines carbon dioxide with hydrogen to form methane and water, usually with a catalyst and controlled temperature. It rearranges existing atoms; it does not create a free supply of energy. Producing the hydrogen may require substantial electricity. Corebound uses the reaction as a useful link between air treatment and fuel production. Leaking methane or making hydrogen with polluting energy would undermine the environmental benefit.
Equation: CO₂ + 4H₂ → CH₄ + 2H₂O
Real world: The International Space Station uses Sabatier technology to recover water from carbon dioxide and hydrogen.

## liquid_drill
Title: Reaching fluids below the ground
Card: A borehole can reach water or oil held in pores and fractures underground. A pump moves the fluid when natural pressure is insufficient. Finding a reservoir is different from finding a limitless tank: withdrawal can change pressure and affect nearby supplies. Corebound simplifies extraction into a steady production machine. Real wells need suitable casing, monitoring and leak prevention, and water must be tested and treated for its intended use.
Equation: —
Real world: Borehole pumps supply groundwater for drinking-water systems and irrigation.

Corebound combines several routes into one production system. Real crude oil is a fossil feedstock, not a renewable biofuel simply because it enters this machine. Land use, process energy and emissions determine how beneficial a biological fuel is.

## biofuel_power_station

Card: A power station can release energy from biological fuels and turn part of it into electricity. Burning these fuels still produces emissions, including carbon dioxide; plant origin does not make a chimney pollution-free. Regrowth, feedstock collection, processing and transport all matter to the overall impact. Corebound treats biofuel as a cleaner option than coal, but that comparison is a gameplay simplification rather than a guarantee for every real project.

## windmill
Title: Borrowing energy from moving air
Card: Wind carries kinetic energy. A turbine takes some of it through shaped blades, turning a shaft connected to a generator. Output changes with wind conditions, and not every location is suitable. Storage or other generators help when wind is weak. Turbines do not burn fuel while generating, but construction, maintenance and siting still matter. Careful planning considers nearby people, habitats and wildlife as well as the electricity produced.

Real world: Onshore and offshore wind turbines supply electricity to power grids.

Real projects must manage water chemistry, dissolved gases and reservoir behaviour; reinjecting used fluid can help maintain the system.

Corebound groups vehicle preparation into a compact structure. Real launch systems need extensive engineering, and their environmental effects depend on the propellants and the launch operation.

## space_elevator
Title: A proposed road to orbit
Card: A space elevator is a proposed way to move cargo along a long tether instead of launching every load on a rocket. An Earth-based design would need a tether extending beyond geostationary orbit, kept under tension as the system rotates. Materials, deployment and operation present major unsolved engineering challenges. Corebound imagines these challenges have been overcome. The climber would still need energy; a cable does not make lifting cargo free.

## ice_water
Title: The same molecule in different forms
Card: Ice and liquid water contain the same molecules, H₂O, arranged and moving differently. Adding heat can melt ice without splitting those molecules into new substances. Electrolysis is a separate chemical process that needs electrical energy to break water into hydrogen and oxygen. Corebound uses local ice as a convenient supply. Real water can contain salts and other impurities, so melting it alone does not guarantee that it is suitable for every use.
Equation: 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (electrolysis, not melting)
Real world: Glaciers store freshwater on Earth as ice.

Richness matters, but so do energy, transport and processing costs. Corebound makes rust-red deposits easy to recognise and mine. Real ore cannot be turned into a useful iron plate by simply melting away the rock: chemical reduction and further processing are generally required.

## silica
Title: Useful oxygen joined to silicon
Card: Silica is silicon dioxide, a compound containing silicon and oxygen. Quartz is a familiar crystalline form, and many sands contain abundant quartz grains. Sand composition varies, so not every pale grain is pure silica. Glassmaking can use silica without first turning it into elemental silicon. Solar-cell production follows a different, longer route involving reduction and purification. Corebound combines these material chains into a small set of useful resources.
Equation: SiO₂
Real world: Quartz-rich sand is an important raw material for making many types of glass.

## carbon
Title: One element with many arrangements
Card: Carbon atoms can join in different structures with very different properties. Graphite has layers that slide easily, while diamond has a strong three-dimensional network. Coal is a mixture rich in carbon, not a block of perfectly pure element. Corebound uses one carbon item for fuel and industrial reduction. Burning it transfers carbon into gases such as carbon dioxide, so the atoms continue through the environment rather than vanishing.
Equation: C + O₂ → CO₂
Real world: Graphite is used in pencil cores, usually mixed with clay.

## copper

Card: Copper is a metal that conducts electricity and heat well. It can also be shaped into wires and other useful forms without breaking easily. These properties make it valuable in electrical equipment. Extracting and refining it uses energy and can generate waste that needs management. Recycling copper keeps useful material in circulation, although collection and processing still take work. A shiny metal can have a long industrial journey behind it.

Remelting collected aluminium generally needs far less energy than producing primary metal from ore. Corebound treats it as another useful construction resource, while real recycling also involves sorting and quality control.

Corebound can use it for advanced equipment, but real engineers choose materials for the whole job, including cost, temperature, fabrication and maintenance, rather than strength alone.

It is dense, soft in pure form and expensive to obtain, so it is not a universal construction material. Mining and processing can have substantial environmental impacts. ## oil
Title: A mixture from deep time
Card: Crude oil is a complex mixture dominated by hydrocarbons, molecules built mainly from hydrogen and carbon. Much of it formed from ancient organic material transformed by burial and heat over geological time. Refineries separate and convert its components into fuels and chemical feedstocks. Burning oil-based fuels releases carbon dioxide, while spills can contaminate land and water. Corebound simplifies extraction and processing, but real oil deposits are finite resources.
Equation: —
Real world: Petroleum refineries supply feedstocks for fuels, lubricants and many chemical products.

## hydrogen
Title: A useful carrier of energy
Card: Hydrogen is the lightest element. As a fuel it usually needs to be produced, stored and transported before use; it is an energy carrier, not an automatic source of free power. Splitting water consumes energy, and using the resulting hydrogen can return only part of that input through a practical system. Its environmental impact depends on the production route. Corebound links it to electrolysis and later fuel chemistry.
Equation: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
Real world: Hydrogen is used in industrial ammonia production.

## oxygen
Title: An essential part of breathing
Card: Oxygen supports the reactions through which our bodies release usable energy from food. It also supports combustion, but it is not itself a fuel. Earth air is a mixture of gases, not pure oxygen, and real habitats must control its composition and pressure. Corebound represents breathing needs with an oxygen meter. The game simplifies carbon dioxide removal, humidity control and other tasks that keep a real life-support system habitable.
Equation: —
Real world: Oxygen makes up about 21 percent of dry air near Earth’s surface.

## methane
Title: Small molecule, important consequences
Card: Methane contains one carbon atom bonded to four hydrogen atoms. Burning it with enough oxygen releases energy and produces carbon dioxide and water. Methane can also escape without burning, and in the atmosphere it is a powerful greenhouse gas. That makes leak prevention important. Corebound can make it through the Sabatier reaction, but producing a useful fuel does not automatically make the whole process clean or energy-positive.
Equation: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O
Real world: Methane is the main component of natural gas.

## co2
Title: Carbon moving through the atmosphere
Card: Carbon dioxide contains one carbon atom and two oxygen atoms. Respiration, combustion and other processes release it, while photosynthesis uses it to build organic matter. It also absorbs outgoing infrared radiation, contributing to the greenhouse effect. That natural role does not make additional emissions harmless. Corebound groups several kinds of atmospheric stress into a readable air indicator; real carbon dioxide and visible smoke are different things with different effects.
Equation: CO₂
Real world: Scientists measure atmospheric carbon dioxide to track changes in Earth’s carbon cycle.
