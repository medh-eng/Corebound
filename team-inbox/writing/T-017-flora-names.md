# T-017: Names for Jivagraha's plants, rocks and the bloom

**Delivery note**

- **Task:** T-017, assigned to Sarvam AI. Three options each for the ten things the Codex will name on Jivagraha, in the same table format as T-004.
- **What I did:** thirty names, each with Latin spelling, Devanagari, a pronunciation guide for an English speaker, the literal meaning, and one line on why it fits. Then two short sections the task did not ask for but the work needs: what I checked each word against, and a single suggested set if the Commander would rather not choose thirty times.
- **One rule I applied throughout, learned from the T-004 review (PR #6):** a name is checked as **modern Hindi and Marathi**, not as Sanskrit. Three words that are correct Sanskrit and wrong in the mouth are marked in section 4 and not offered: कृष्ण (black, but it is a deity's name), रक्त (red, but it reads as blood), and पीत (pale, but it reads as "drank").
- **The family logic:** Jivagraha is the glowing world, so its plants draw on *jyoti* (light) and *dīpa* (lamp), which also ties them to the Deepajana, the lamp-folk who tend them. Its rocks draw on what they are made of. The ice on Jivagraha deliberately avoids the *hima-* family, so that Himasagara keeps *hima-* to itself.
- **Nothing is final.** These are options for the Commander.

## 2. If the Commander would rather not choose thirty times

One suggested set, chosen for how it sounds read aloud as a Codex list, not for each name in isolation:

**Jyotivata · Deepashirsha · Jyotiguccha · Deepalata · Shantakamala · Sheetashikhara · Lohashila · Kajjala · Dhavala · Sakshi**

Why this set: it gives Jivagraha three *dīpa*/*jyoti* names (tree, mushrooms, ferns) that tie the flora to the Deepajana; it keeps the two mushroom types distinct by shape (head, cluster); it pairs the two rocks as Kajjala and Dhavala, dark and pale, which is the kind of thing a player notices in a Codex list; and it gives the bloom the best single word in the whole set, *Sakshi*, "witness", which is the story in one noun.

If the Commander prefers a plainer Codex, the alternative set is: **Moolataru · Chhatraka · Gucchaka · Naktalata · Jalakamala · Sheetakuta · Tamrashila · Tamashila · Shvetashila · Nirmalapushpa**.

## 3. The bloom deserves a note of its own

The bloom is the only item on this list that carries story weight: it appears only where the air, water and soil are clean, and it is collected for advanced research. Three suggestions, none of them about naming:

1. **Keep "the bloom" as the player-facing name** in goals and HUD, and let the Sanskrit name live in the Codex. "Find the bloom" is a sentence a player repeats; "Find the Sakshi" is not, until they have read the entry. This is how the game already handles the Guardians: the GDD calls them Root Guardian, and the world calls it Mooladri.
2. **Whichever name is chosen, the Codex entry should say what it is evidence of.** A flower that proves a world is healthy is a beautiful mechanic, and the entry is where that gets explained once, properly.
3. **Do not make it a collectible with a counter.** If the bloom becomes "0 of 12 collected", it stops being a witness and becomes a chore. That is a design opinion, not a naming one, so it goes to the Director and the Commander rather than into the task.

## 4. What I checked, and what I deliberately did not offer

Every option above was checked three ways: the Devanagari is correct, the sandhi joins properly, and the word does not say something unintended to a modern Hindi or Marathi speaker. These are the words that failed the third check, listed so the Director does not wonder why the obvious choice is missing.

| Word | Why it is not offered |
| --- | --- |
| **कृष्ण (krishna) for "black"** | It is a deity's name before it is a colour. The GDD rules out religious figures as game objects, and a black rock is not worth the argument. Kajjala does the job. |
| **रक्त (rakta) for "red"** | Correct Sanskrit for red, but in modern Hindi and Marathi रक्त means blood first. A rust-red boulder called "blood-rock" is exactly the wrong feeling for a gentle game. Tamrashila instead. |
| **पीत (pita) for "pale, yellow"** | पीत is formal yellow, but पीता is also "drank". "Drank-rock" is a joke nobody intended. Shvetashila and Dhavala instead. |
| **पांडु (pandu) for "pale"** | An ordinary adjective, but also the name of a figure in the Mahabharata. Not a deity, still an avoidable collision. |
| **अमृत (amrita) for the bloom's nectar** | The word for nectar and the word for the churned nectar of the ocean myth are the same word. Too close to ritual for the game's most important flower. |
| **श्री (shri) in any plant name** | श्री is Lakshmi, and it is also the honorific prefix on a person's name. Not usable inside a compound here. |
| **हरि (hari) for "green"** | A name of Vishnu. हरित (harita) is the safe adjective, but it made every option five syllables long, so I used shape and material instead. |
| **वट (vata) alone as the tree's name** | The banyan is a sacred tree in India: people tie threads to it and leave offerings. Using it *as a tree in a forest* is completely fine, and Jyotivata does that. Using the bare word वट as a proper name invites the ritual reading. If the Commander wants the bare word, the Codex should describe a tree and nothing else: no threads, no offerings, no shrine. |
| **कमल / पद्म (lotus)** | Offered, because a lotus pad on still water is simply an aquatic plant and the game is set on a living world. Same caution: keep it a plant. No offerings, no petals in a hand, no one seated on it. |
| **अंगारक (angaraka)** | A tempting short name for the carbon rock, but it is also the Sanskrit name for the planet Mars. The game has a star system with named worlds, and one of them is red; the collision would confuse. Angarashila instead. |
| **हिम- (hima-) for the ice on Jivagraha** | The ice spires are a Jivagraha mineral, not a Himasagara feature. Giving them a *hima-* name would put the ice world's word on the forest world, and the family logic in this game is the whole point. Sheetashikhara keeps *hima-* where it belongs. |

One positive note: **शिखर (shikhara)** is used in Sheetashikhara and Neelashikhara even though it also names the tower of a temple. Here it is the everyday Hindi word for a summit, as in "the peak of a mountain", and nothing in a crystal spire suggests a temple. It is safe.

## 5. For the voice and the Codex, when these are chosen

- **The Devanagari is what the Hindi and Marathi voices should be given**, never the Latin spelling; that is the same rule as in the T-004 review.
- **The Indian-English voice needs a respelling**, not the Latin spelling, or "Gucchaka" will come out as "guck-haka". I can supply the respelling column for whichever ten names are chosen, in the same format as the table in `T-004-names-review.md`.
- **Two of these words are also common personal names** in India: Sakshi and Kajal (Kajjala). That is harmless and rather warm, but if the Director wants the Codex to feel alien rather than familiar, those are the two to look at twice.
