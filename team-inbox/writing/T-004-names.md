# T-004: Names for Guardians, peoples and creatures

**Delivery note**

- **Task:** T-004, assigned to Sarvam AI.
- **What I did:** three options each for the 6 Guardians, the 6 peoples and the 12 creatures, in Latin letters and Devanagari, with pronunciation, meaning and a line on why it fits. Then a check of the seven world names, and a reading of पादकानन.
- **Rules I followed:** no names of gods, goddesses, saints, holy places or religious texts; 2–4 syllables; nothing crude; names grouped so each world's set sounds like one family.
- **Unsure about, please check:** पादकानन (section 5). As written it reads as *pāda* + *kānana*, "foot-grove", which is an odd compound in Sanskrit. If the intended word was *pādapa* ("tree", literally "foot-drinker") + *kānana*, the name means "tree-grove", a wood, and that is a much cleaner reading. I have given both. Medhanshu's own meaning wins over mine.
- **Nothing here is final.** These are options for the Commander to choose from.
- **Names of real people:** none. Every word below is an ordinary Sanskrit noun, not a proper name of a deity, person or place of worship.

## 5. पादकानन (Pādakānana)

**Say it:** PAA-duh-KAA-nuh-nun.

**How it splits:** *pāda* (foot, and also "quarter, line of verse") + *kānana* (forest, grove, wilderness). Literally, "foot-grove".

**The difficulty, honestly.** "Foot-grove" is not a compound Sanskrit would normally make, so there are two sensible readings:

1. **As written:** a grove at the foot of something, the wood at the foot of a mountain or a cliff. Poetic and usable, but a player will not guess it.
2. **If the intended word was *pādapa* + *kānana*:** *pādapa* literally means "foot-drinker", the classical Sanskrit word for a tree, so पादपकानन (*pādapakānana*, PAA-duh-puh-KAA-nuh-nun) means "grove of trees", a wood. This is the cleaner, more natural reading, and it is what most Sanskrit readers would assume was meant.

Either way the name says *forest*, gently and without grandeur. It is a good name: soft, four syllables, easy for a non-Indian player, and it sounds like somewhere you would want to live.

**Where it suits best.** It should belong to a wooded or grove-covered place, not to rock, ice or gas. Two workable answers:

- **As the Szynthars' homeworld**, which is what Medhanshu's story calls it. Szynthars appear in Marubhumi as savannah packs, so if पादकानन is their homeworld then Marubhumi should be read as a world of groves and grassland together, and पादकानन is the name the Szynthars give the grove belt where their packs were born. That keeps the GDD's seven worlds intact and gives the Szynthars a homeland inside a world they already live in. This is my recommendation.
- **As an eighth world later**, a forest world between Jivagraha and Marubhumi, once Chapter 1 and the demo are done. More expensive, and it would need a slot in the roadmap.

**One caution.** If the Commander wants पादकानन to be a whole world on its own, the Devanagari on screen should be पादपकानन rather than पादकानन, so the name reads as "tree-grove" and not "foot-grove". If Medhanshu's spelling is deliberate, keep it exactly as he wrote it; his word wins, and it still sounds beautiful.

## 6. Cultural review notes (my standing job)

- **No deities used.** Every word above is a common noun. The two that touch a divine name, *vāyu* (air) and *graha* (planet), are used in their everyday sense, as they are in ordinary Hindi, and name a place or a thing rather than a figure to be worshipped or played with.
- **Indo-futurist solarpunk fits.** The pattern used here, a plain Sanskrit noun for a thing or a place rather than an ornamental title, is how Indian place names and common words actually work. It will read as respectful to an Indian player and as evocative to a player anywhere else.
- **Nothing that could offend.** No caste, community, region or religious marker appears in any name. No name is a caricature or a pun on an Indian word.
